Abstract

Transparent surfaces are ubiquitous in built environments, yet they remain a persistent failure case for robotic perception. RGB cameras perceive the background behind glass rather than the surface itself, while depth sensors such as LiDAR, time-of-flight, and RGB-D often return invalid or background measurements in transparent regions.

We propose a multimodal framework that fuses millimetre-wave radar with RGB-D sensing for real-time transparent surface segmentation. Radar reflects strongly off glass surfaces, providing a geometric cue that remains reliable precisely where vision and depth fail. We exploit this cross-modal inconsistency to generate a radar-guided spatial prior, integrated into a lightweight transformer-based segmentation network, GlassFormer, via cross-modal attention. GlassFormer achieves 0.88 mIoU on the mixed split and 0.59 mIoU on the low-light split, with real-time performance on resource-constrained platforms.

Method

A mmWave pulsed coherent radar estimates the range of dominant scene reflectors. This range is projected into the image plane and fused with RGB-D depth inconsistency cues to generate a coarse spatial prior localizing transparent surface candidates. The prior is injected into a SegFormer-B2 backbone through a cross-modal RadarAttention module that modulates deep semantic features with range-conditioned spatial context.

Dataset

A synchronized RGB-D-radar dataset of 1800 annotated frames spanning glass doors, windows, full-length walls, mirrors, tinted and partially occluded glass, captured across lighting conditions from daylight to near-dark. Collected with an Acconeer XM125 pulsed coherent radar rigidly mounted to an Intel RealSense D455 via a 3D-printed bracket. Download link coming soon.

Code

Training, evaluation, the GlassFormer model, and an open-source ROS2 driver for the Acconeer XM125 (with a real-time inference node) are available on GitHub.
